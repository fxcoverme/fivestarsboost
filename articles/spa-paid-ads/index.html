Paid ads for spas live or die on simple math: the average spa visit is worth $110. A campaign that brings in three extra guests a week more than pays for itself — and a campaign aimed at the wrong searches quietly spends the same money on people who will never book.

Which platform fits a spa

Google comes first for most spas. “Massage near me” is a search with a wallet attached — someone in pain or planning a gift, ready to book today. Instagram fits the visual side: treatment rooms, seasonal packages, and gift-card season. TikTok can work for distinctive treatments, but it skews toward browsers over bookers. For a single-location spa, the honest order is Google to capture the demand that already exists in your zip codes, then social to create more of it — not the reverse.

Running it yourself vs having it managed

The spa owner's problem is time: your hands are on clients all day, and the phone is at the front desk. DIY campaigns get built once and never pruned, so the budget leaks into searches for jobs, do-it-yourself massage tips, and browsers three towns away. Managed campaigns tighten the radius to real drive time, read the actual search terms every week, and track calls — so you know which ad produced a booking. With a $110 average visit, a handful of wasted clicks a day is a treatment's worth of revenue walking out monthly.
