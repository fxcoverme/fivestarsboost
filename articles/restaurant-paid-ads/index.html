Paid Ads for Restaurants: TikTok, Instagram, or Google?

Paid ads for restaurants split into two jobs: making people hungry, and catching people already deciding where to eat tonight. TikTok and Instagram do the first; Google does the second. Buying only one is paying for half a funnel.

Which platform fits a restaurant

TikTok and Instagram are discovery machines: dishes travel by video, and a thirty-second clip of a signature plate does what no text ad can — filling weekends and launching new menu items. Google is intent: “best ramen near me” typed by someone who will eat somewhere within the hour. Fewer impressions, far more intent — and your listing's stars sit right next to the ad. Meta also retargets people who already viewed your menu, the cheapest audience you own. A practical order: if weeknights are empty, fund Google first; if the food photographs beautifully and weekends already queue, social video builds the waitlist.

Running it yourself vs having it managed

The boost button is where restaurant ad budgets go to die: it targets “engagement” — likes from anyone, anywhere — not diners within driving distance deciding tonight. Managed campaigns set a realistic radius, run before lunch and dinner decision hours instead of at 2 a.m., and track calls and direction requests rather than hearts. The other classic leak is geography: ads shown across a whole metro to people who will never drive forty minutes for dinner.
