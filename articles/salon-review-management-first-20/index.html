Salon Review Management: Crossing the 20-Review Line That Decides Bookings

Half of personal-care businesses have fewer than 20 reviews — and 47% of consumers will not use a business under that line. Salon review management is how a good salon crosses it: not with a one-off push, but with replies that make clients want to write the next one.

What the data says about nail salon reviews

47%

73%

Salons already do the hard part — the work photographs beautifully and clients leave happy. The missing piece is the last step: every review answered personally, within a day, so the page that new clients check before booking shows a salon that notices people. That is salon reputation management at its simplest.

A case we measured

A hair and nail salon with 17 Google reviews and a 4.8-star rating — excellent work, invisible business. Of its 10 most recent reviews, 6 had no reply at all, and the newest review was two months old. The salon asks happy clients to post, and sometimes they do. With no answers waiting for them, most never get around to it.

A thank-you that invites the next review sounds like this:

"Thank you, Priya — we're so glad the balayage came out exactly how you pictured it. We'll pass your words to Ana, and we can't wait to see you for your next appointment."

Named, specific, and warm — future clients reading it can picture their own thank-you waiting, and that picture is what gets reviews written.
