How to Respond to a Negative Review: A Med Spa Owner's Guide

Only 9.8% of 1-star reviews ever receive a reply — and the average one waits 19.5 days for it. Learning how to respond to a negative review is the highest-value skill in med spa reputation work, because the reader is never the reviewer. It is the next client, mid-research, deciding whether a $527 visit (industry average, for illustration) feels safe.

What the data says about medical spa reviews

Most med spas are single-location businesses — 81% — where the owner is often the injector. There is no marketing team to draft the hard replies, so the hard replies do not get drafted. That gap, not the treatment menu, is what aesthetic practice reviews usually expose.

A case we measured

A med spa with 188 Google reviews and a 4.6-star rating. Of its 10 most recent reviews, 5 had no reply at all — including a 2-star review saying results 'weren't worth the money.' That sentence, unanswered, is what every researching client weighs against the price list.

A reply that protects the next booking sounds like this:

"Thank you for sharing this honestly — results should always feel worth it, and we're sorry yours didn't. Treatment outcomes vary, and we'd genuinely like to review your plan with you in person and talk through options. Please call us so we can arrange that."

No promises, no defensiveness, no template — and every future client sees a practice that stands behind its work.
