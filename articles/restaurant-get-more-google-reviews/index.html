Get More Google Reviews: The Restaurant Ask That Actually Works

Restaurants live on volume: the typical listing in Google Maps carries 216 reviews, and the median in the top three results is 451. If you want to get more Google reviews, the ask matters — but what happens after the review lands matters more.

What the data says about Google reviews for restaurants

+12%

more new reviews, on average, for businesses that reply to the reviews they receive. Replying is the quiet engine behind asking.

47%

of consumers will not use a business with fewer than 20 reviews. Below that line, even a perfect ask cannot put you on the shortlist.

89%

of consumers expect the owner to respond to reviews. A guest who sees replies knows their own review will be read — so they write it.

5–9%

more revenue for each additional star at independent restaurants (Harvard Business School research by Michael Luca). Volume feeds the rating that feeds the till.

The ask itself is simple: a genuine, well-timed request at the end of a good meal, from the person who served it. What most restaurants miss is the loop — every review answered within a day, by name where possible, so the next guest reading the page sees a conversation, not a noticeboard. That loop is what respond to restaurant reviews really means as a habit, not a chore.

A case we measured

A family restaurant with 96 Google reviews and a 4.3-star rating. Of its 10 most recent reviews, 8 had no reply at all — including four five-star reviews naming dishes and servers. The owner asks for reviews on the receipt and at the till, and guests do write them. But the page never answers back, and the flow of new reviews has thinned to a couple a month.

A five-star thank-you answered personally sounds like this:

"Thank you, Daniel — the lamb shank is our chef's favourite too, and we'll tell Rosa you mentioned her. See you next time you're in the neighbourhood."

One specific sentence tells every future guest: write here, and a person reads it. That is how review counts climb.
