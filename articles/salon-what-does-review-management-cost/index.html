What Does Review Management Cost? What Salons Actually Pay For

73% of personal-care businesses ask for reviews regularly — but only 57% of reviews get a reply. So when owners ask what does review management cost, the honest answer starts with the other cost: reviews you worked to earn, sitting unanswered while new clients decide.

What you pay for in a service is not software or a dashboard. It is every review answered within one business day, in your own voice, on every platform — plus the hard reviews handled carefully instead of at midnight. Salon review management should be judged against the booking it protects: 68% of consumers require at least 4 stars, and 31% require at least 4.5.

A case we measured

A hair and nail salon with 64 Google reviews and a 4.6-star rating. Of its 10 most recent reviews, 6 had no reply at all — including praise for two stylists by name, and one complaint about a booking mix-up. The team had asked for every one of those reviews. Nobody had time to answer them, so the page looked cared for by clients and ignored by the salon.

A reply costs little and changes the story. A good answer is two sentences:

""We're sorry we mixed up your appointment time — that's on us, and we've changed how we confirm bookings. Please call us and we'll get you rebooked at a time that suits you.""

One specific apology protects more future bookings than a week of posts — because new clients read replies before they book.
